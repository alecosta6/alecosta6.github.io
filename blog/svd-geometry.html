Singular Value Decomposition (SVD): Geometry of the Four Fundamental Subspaces

Alessio Costa January 2026 6 min read Linear Algebra

The Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is arguably the crowning jewel of linear algebra and scientific computing. Unlike eigenvalue decomposition (which is restricted to square diagonalizable matrices), the SVD exists for any matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$.

1. The Decomposition Theorem

Any real matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ of rank $r \le \min(m, n)$ can be factored into the product of three canonical matrices:

$$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T = \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T$$

where:

  • $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ is an orthogonal matrix whose columns $\mathbf{u}_i$ are the left-singular vectors (orthonormal eigenvectors of $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^T$).
  • $\mathbf{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is a diagonal matrix containing the singular values in descending order: $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0$.
  • $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is an orthogonal matrix whose columns $\mathbf{v}_i$ are the right-singular vectors (orthonormal eigenvectors of $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}$).

2. Geometric Action: Sphere to Ellipsoid

The SVD provides an intuitive geometric decomposition of linear mappings:

Geometric Decomposition
Any linear transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$ decomposes into three simple geometric steps:
  1. $\mathbf{V}^T$: An isometric rotation/reflection in the domain $\mathbb{R}^n$.
  2. $\mathbf{\Sigma}$: An axis-aligned stretching/shrinking by singular values $\sigma_i$.
  3. $\mathbf{U}$: An isometric rotation/reflection in the codomain $\mathbb{R}^m$.
Consequently, the unit sphere $S = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = 1 \}$ is mapped to a hyperellipsoid with principal semi-axes lengths $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r$ oriented along vectors $\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_r$.

3. The Four Fundamental Subspaces

The singular vectors form the ideal orthonormal bases for the four fundamental subspaces of Gilbert Strang's framework:

  • Row Space $\operatorname{Row}(\mathbf{A}) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_r) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$
  • Nullspace $\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf{A}) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{v}_{r+1}, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$
  • Column Space $\operatorname{Col}(\mathbf{A}) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_r) \subset \mathbb{R}^m$
  • Left Nullspace $\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf{A}^T) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{u}_{r+1}, \dots, \mathbf{u}_m) \subset \mathbb{R}^m$

4. Low-Rank Approximation (Eckart-Young-Mirsky)

The optimal rank-$k$ approximation of $\mathbf{A}$ under both the Frobenius and spectral matrix norms is obtained simply by truncating the SVD at the first $k < r$ terms:

$$\mathbf{A}_k = \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T, \quad \|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$$
